RECONCILIATION DAY — THE RECAPEVERY CHILD MATTERS.

RECONCILIATION DAY 2026: THE RECAP + THE JOURNEY

OCT 5, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

We were downtown Toronto for the National Day for Truth and Reconciliation — and the recap is finally here. 🧡 Fifty-six seconds: the teepees, the orange shirts, the gathering. Every child matters.

Let me tell you who I am, in my own words. I'm Métis Indigenous — Eastern Woodlands, Nova Scotia roots. And right now I'm on a journey to get my status card in Nova Scotia, where my people come from.

Here's what they don't tell you: something so simple, they make it so hard. Ontario won't recognize my nation. The Métis Nation of Ontario only recognizes certain communities — if your roots aren't on their list, you don't exist to them. My roots are Nova Scotia. Eastern Woodlands. That's who I am, and no office gets to vote on that.

So I keep fighting. Not just for me — for the children. Jordan's Principle says no child should get caught between jurisdictions, waiting while governments argue over whose responsibility they are. Every child deserves what they need, when they need it. That's not a slogan to me. That's personal.

My dad's birthday was October 1st and mine October 2nd. I was out in the festivities gaining awareness — because as you know, my dad is sick in the hospital. And here's my fight inside the fight: he is not on my birth certificate, so I'm having a hard time getting my native status. Something that should be simple, made hard. But reconciliation is what we fight for. The journey never stops. 🧡

CONWAY THE MACHINE AT THE OPERA HOUSE — THE SHOW THAT ALMOST DIDN'T HAPPEN (TWICE)

OCT 7, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

November 1, 2023. The Opera House, 735 Queen East, Toronto. Conway the Machine — Won't He Do It Tour, his second-ever Toronto show. And this show almost didn't happen. Twice. Back in June, Conway broke his leg and pulled out of the same venue — Ghostface Killah filled in last minute. Then the rescheduled October date got cancelled too. When Conway finally walked out that November night, straight off his European tour and still healing, he opened on a cane — then ditched the cane mid-set and finished the night on his feet.

And I was in the room with the camera. The footage above is mine — thirteen minutes, uploaded the next day to the UrbanXklusives channel. Run it up.

Toronto held the undercard down heavy: Lord Juco with the punchline set, Asun Eastwood — a Toronto emcee with a Conway feature going back to 2016, on stage at Conway's Toronto show seven years later, full circle — plus Saipher Soze repping Brampton, MBK Supreme, and Goosebytheway as Conway's official Drumwork opener, with Lord Fury's Supreme Genes putting the whole night together. Buffalo to Toronto, one stage. The cross-border underground circuit, documented.

That's what this site is: the documentary. I don't just post the culture — I film it, I was there. Press play, and if you haven't yet, subscribe to the channel — more footage from the vault is coming. #URXKLUSIVEKONNECT

Toronto's own Ladji Mouflet just dropped a warrior record. "Hatuey" is baile, Afro percussion and electronic built for the dancefloor — but it carries something much older underneath. It's named for the Taíno chief who chose defiance over surrender, and Ladji carries Taíno heritage through his own mother's lineage. "Baiana 2022" did 35 million. "French Bossa Nova" did 44 million. This is what the independent grind looks like when it wins.
