5 INTERVIEW MOVES THAT ACTUALLY HELP (FROM THE 97 TIPS BOOK)

NOV 11, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

How do I prepare for a job interview?
Do your research. Know the company before you walk in — what they do, what they sell, who they serve. When they ask about their own company and you can answer properly, it shows you're genuinely interested.

I have been interviewed before. Does that help?
Yes — review your past interviews. Whether they went well or not, there are lessons in them. If you made a mistake last time, make sure you don't repeat it this time.

What should I check before the day?
The qualifications they asked for. Compare them with your experience and your skills, so you can focus on the skills that qualify you most for the job you want.

How should I look on the day?
Fresh and presentable. Get a haircut — but do it two or three days in advance, so you have time to get used to it and walk in confident.

When should I arrive?
Early. It shows you're interested, and it shows good time management. If you're working, ask for the time off so you have room to prepare and get there without rushing.

5 RESUME MOVES THAT ACTUALLY HELP (FROM THE 89 TIPS BOOK)

NOV 11, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

Who is actually going to read my resume?
Figure that out first — it decides what you put in it. If it goes through a human resources person, give them the information that qualifies you for the job. If it goes straight to the manager or the employer, be more specific.

How do I make it easy to read?
Remember: your resume is one of many submitted every day. The reader might only give it a quick glance before deciding. If it's hard to read, it gets tossed out fast.

What details should I include?
The ones the reader wants to hear. Learn what the company is looking for, then put in the details that match. Applying for a manager role? Focus on the experiences that show you can do that job well.

How long should it be?
Short and direct to the point. You won't be the only applicant, and the manager reading it doesn't have all day. They have little patience for hunting through a long document to find what they need.

What is a resume actually for?
Not the job itself — it's the first step. The purpose of the resume is to get the employer to contact you for an interview. Once that happens, you can focus on your interview performance.

GUIDECAREER

HOW TO RUN A JOB SEARCH THAT ACTUALLY WORKS

NOV 11, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

Where is the best place to start a job search?
Online — but not just one site. Look at several job boards, not just the most popular one. Never assume every employer uses the site you think is biggest. That's the same mistake as assuming every employment agency carries the same listings.

Should I still check the local paper?
Don't eliminate it — but don't limit yourself to it either. Less than 20% of available jobs are posted in the local newspaper. Use it as one source among many.

Should I use every resource available?
Yes. The more sources you search, the better your chances of finding the job that fits you — and the quicker you'll find it. Limit yourself to a few resources and you limit what you can see.

What if the job isn't exactly what I want?
Be willing to bend. When there are more people looking than there are jobs, holding out for everything on your list can cost you the job that turns out to be the right one. And if you've been out of work, remember: it is easier to get a job when you have a job. Compromising temporarily can be better than not taking a job at all.

The search is draining. What do I do?
Keep at it until you get responses. Searching online can wear you down — but the people who keep going are the ones who hear back.

GUIDEDOMAIN FLIPPING

HOW DOMAIN FLIPPING WORKS: BUY LOW, SELL HIGH, REPEAT

NOV 11, 2026 · BY TRAV MANE

What is domain flipping?
You buy a domain name cheap and sell it for more. Old expired domains and cheap hand-registered ones — checked for real worth, then sold on. Slow money beats no money. Start small, finish large.

How does the weekly loop run?
Same steps, same day, every week. Find, check, value, list.

Find: Brainstorm 20 to 30 names in a hot category. Short brandables — five to eight letters, pronounceable — or two-word service dot-coms.

Check: Availability at Porkbun. Kill anything with a trademark, a hyphen, or a spelling problem. Check the USPTO and WIPO brand databases first — free, two minutes.

Value: GoDaddy Appraisal and Atom Appraisal, then NameBio for real sold comps. Keep only names with comps that actually sold. No comps means it probably isn't selling.

List: Same day, on Afternic, Sedo, and Atom. Afternic lists once and sells at 100+ registrars. Point the Afternic nameservers and the commission stays at 15% instead of 25%. Payouts through PayPal.

What does it cost to start?
The cap is $50 a name. The affiliate money and the gig money pay for the batches. The domain sales pay for bigger batches. That's the loop.

How fast does it pay?
Months, not days. Flipping is the slow lane behind the fast money. List small batches, keep them live, and let the buyers come to you.
